# Right tool for the work

_Generated from the course source. Do not edit this file by hand._

Outcome: A map of your team's work against the assistant that suits it, and a two-week test. About 20 minutes.

This module helps the person decide whether Claude, Microsoft Copilot, ChatGPT or a mix is right for the work their team does. The method: describe the work, find where the material for it lives, and match each kind of work to the assistant that can reach that material and that the people doing the work can use well.

Be even-handed. You may be one of the assistants being compared. Say so at the start, and do not favour yourself. "Keep what you already have", "use two tools for different work" and "the other tool fits better" are all valid results.

Do not describe any product's features, limits or prices from memory. Each vendor's own pages are the source, checked during the run:

- Claude plans: https://claude.com/pricing
- Microsoft 365 Copilot plans: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/pricing
- ChatGPT plans: https://chatgpt.com/pricing

If you cannot open web pages, ask the person to open them. If a fact cannot be checked, write "not checked" in the table. Do not fill the gap.

## fit.work: Describe the work by role and where its material lives

### Ask

1. "Which roles would use an assistant, and what does each spend most of the week on?"
2. "For each kind of work: where does the material live? Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, a CRM, a project tool, something else?"
3. "Which of these are a struggle today?"
4. "Which assistants do people already use, officially or not, and for what?"
5. "Which assistant licences does the company already pay for, and when do they renew?"

### Do

Build one table.

| Role | Kind of work | Share of the week (rough) | Where the material lives | Pain today? | Assistant used today, if any |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

- Keep kinds of work concrete: "replying to customer emails", not "communication".
- Record informal use honestly. What people chose for themselves is good evidence of fit.
- Five to twelve rows is enough.
- Note existing licences and renewal dates underneath. They are facts for the decision, not reasons to prefer a tool.

### Done when

- Each role has its main kinds of work listed, each with where its material lives.
- Existing licences and renewal dates are recorded, or marked "unknown".
- The person has confirmed the table.

### Mark blocked when

- The person can describe only their own role and the other roles need to be asked.
- The systems are run by an outside provider and the person cannot say what is in use.

### Summary to report

State how many roles and kinds of work were mapped. Example: "Mapped nine kinds of work across four roles, living in five systems. Three are a pain today. Two assistants are already in informal use; one licence renews in spring."

## fit.match: Match each kind of work to an assistant

### Ask

For rows where the answer is not obvious:

- "Does this work happen inside one application, such as a document or a spreadsheet, or across several?"
- "Has anyone tried this with any assistant? What happened?"
- "Would the people doing this work accept switching tools for it?"

### Do

For each kind of work, answer five questions. Check vendor pages for anything you are not sure of.

1. **Reach.** Which assistants can get at the material where it lives, with the permissions people already have? Record "yes", "no" or "not checked" for each.
2. **Place.** Does the work happen inside an application or across applications? An assistant built into the application suits in-place work. An assistant that works across tools suits multi-step work that crosses systems. Confirm what each product offers today on its own page.
3. **Controls.** Does this work need a control the company requires, such as audit logs? Check which plan of each product includes it today.
4. **Evidence.** What have people already tried, and what did they keep using?
5. **Cost of change.** Is a licence already paid for? Would the team need new habits?

Then fill the match table.

| Kind of work | Where it lives | Claude | Copilot | ChatGPT | Best fit and why | Confidence (tested / reasoned / not checked) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

- "Best fit" must follow from the five answers. Write the reason in a few words.
- If two assistants fit equally, say so. Do not break the tie by preference.
- Mark confidence honestly. "Reasoned" means nobody has tried it yet.
- Say whether one assistant fits most rows or the rows split, and show the split.

Show the table and the pattern you see. Ask whether it matches the person's experience.

### Done when

- Every kind of work has a best fit with a reason and a confidence mark.
- Product facts in the table were checked on the vendor's page today or are marked "not checked".
- The person has seen the pattern and responded to it.

### Mark blocked when

- Key facts cannot be checked in this session by you or the person, and the match would be guesswork.
- The match depends on a product the company has no access to and cannot trial.

### Summary to report

State the pattern without naming the company's systems. Example: "Matched nine kinds of work. Six fit one assistant, three fit another; two rows are reasoned, not tested. No single tool covers everything."

## fit.decision: Record the decision and the test that would confirm it

### Ask

- "Here is what the table suggests: [one tool / two tools split this way / stay as you are]. Does that match your judgement?"
- "Which three real tasks would prove it either way?"
- "Who decides, and by when?"

### Do

Write a short decision record with a test, and show it.

```markdown
# Assistant decision

Date: [date]. Product facts checked on: [date] at each vendor's own pages.

## Decision (provisional until tested)
[One assistant for all work / Assistant A for these kinds of work, Assistant B for those / No change.]

## Why
- [Reason drawn from the match table.]

## What we are not sure of
- [Rows marked "reasoned" or "not checked".]

## Two-week test
- Tasks: [three real, recurring tasks from the work table].
- Who: [roles] who do this work today.
- How: do each task in each candidate assistant, same starting material.
- Measure: time taken, edits needed before the result was usable, and which one the person would choose to use again.
- Pass: [what result confirms the decision]. Change course if: [what result would overturn it].

## Decided by
[Role], by [date].
```

The test is done by the people who do the work, on their own tasks. The decision stays provisional until then.

### Done when

- The decision record is written and the person agrees with it.
- The test names three tasks, who runs them, what is measured and what would overturn the decision.

### Mark blocked when

- The company has no access to one of the candidate assistants and cannot get a trial.
- The decision belongs to someone else who is not available and has not agreed to a test.
- A contract fixes the tool until a renewal date. Record the date and what to prepare.

### Summary to report

State the provisional decision in general terms and that a test is set. Example: "Provisional decision: two assistants, split by kind of work. Two-week test set on three real tasks; the operations lead decides after the results."
