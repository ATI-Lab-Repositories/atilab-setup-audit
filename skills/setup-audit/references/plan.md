# Plan and seats

_Generated from the course source. Do not edit this file by hand._

Outcome: A plan recommendation with the reasons written down. About 20 minutes.

This module answers one question: which Claude plan, and how many seats of which kind. The answer comes from who will use Claude and how heavily, which admin and security controls the company must have, and what each option costs today.

Prices, seat limits and plan features change. Do not use numbers from memory or from this file. Read them during the run from Anthropic's own pages:

- Plans and prices: https://claude.com/pricing
- Team plan: https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9266767-what-is-the-team-plan
- Enterprise plan: https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9797531-what-is-the-enterprise-plan
- Usage credits and spend limits: https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12005970-manage-usage-credits-for-team-and-seat-based-enterprise-plans

If you cannot open web pages, ask the person to open the pricing page and read you the figures. Write down the date you checked.

One caution before you start. If the question behind this module is "we keep hitting limits, do we need more seats?", more seats are rarely the answer. Each person has their own allowance. Record the seat count here, and deal with the cause in the `processes` and `connectors` modules.

## plan.needs: Count users by role and list the admin and security controls you need

### Ask

Users first, one question at a time:

1. "How many people use Claude today, on which plan, and how many should be using it in three months?"
2. "Group them by role. For each role: how many people, and is their use occasional, most days, or long working sessions?"
3. "Does anyone share a login today?"

Then controls. For each, the answer is "must have", "nice to have" or "not needed":

4. "Must people sign in with the company login (single sign-on)?"
5. "When someone joins or leaves, must access be added and removed automatically from your staff directory?"
6. "Do you need a record of who did what in the admin settings, or to export conversations for compliance?"
7. "Do you need to set how long chats are kept, or to give different groups different permissions?"
8. "Do you handle health information under HIPAA, or does a client, insurer or regulator set rules on the tools you use?"

### Do

Build the user table. No names.

| Role | People | Main uses | Use level (occasional / daily / heavy) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

- "Heavy" means long sessions, coding tools or multi-step tasks. These people reach limits first, so they matter most for seat type.
- Each seat is for one named person. Shared logins end whichever plan is chosen.

Then list each control with its answer and a reason in a few words. Open the pricing page and the two plan pages. For each "must have", find which plans include it today and record what the page says and the date.

As a guide to what to look for: when this module was written, the pricing page listed single sign-on and spend controls on both Team and Enterprise, and listed automatic provisioning (SCIM), audit logs, the Compliance API, custom data retention, role-based access and a HIPAA-ready option under Enterprise only. Confirm each one on the page today before you rely on it.

If a "must have" is on one plan only, say so plainly. That often decides the plan before cost is considered. You are not giving legal or compliance advice; where a rule comes from a contract or a regulator, record who inside the company owns that question.

### Done when

- The table covers every role that will use Claude, with a head count and a use level.
- Every control has an answer, and each "must have" is matched to the plans that include it, with the date checked.

### Mark blocked when

- The person does not know who uses Claude today and cannot see the member list because they are not an owner.
- The person does not know the company's requirements and whoever does (IT, compliance, a director) is not available.
- The official pages cannot be read by you or by the person in this session.

### Summary to report

State the number of users by use level and the required controls. Example: "22 users in five roles: 12 occasional, 7 daily, 3 heavy. Three controls required; two of them are listed only under Enterprise as of today's check."

## plan.cost: Compare the cost of each plan for your seat count

### Ask

- "Would you pay monthly or annually, and in which country are you billed?"
- If Enterprise is in play: "Has anyone already had a quote from Anthropic?"

### Do

1. Read today's figures from https://claude.com/pricing and the plan pages. For each plan still in play, record the seat types and what each includes, the price per seat for monthly and annual billing, the minimum and maximum number of seats, and how usage beyond the included amount is charged.
2. Check that the seat count fits inside each plan's minimum and maximum. A plan the company does not fit is out; say so.
3. Work out the cost for each plan that fits. Where a plan has more than one seat type, put heavy users on the higher seat and everyone else on the standard seat, and show a second version with everyone on the standard seat. Where a plan charges a seat fee plus usage, show the seat fee as a firm number and the usage as an estimated range, and say that the range is a guess until there is a month of real data.
4. Prices are usually shown before tax and can differ by region. Point the person to the price in their own account before they buy.
5. Present one small table: plan, seat mix, monthly cost, annual cost, what is firm and what is estimated.

Do not round in a way that favours one option. Do not include discounts you have not been told about.

### Done when

- Each plan still in play has a cost for the real seat count, with firm and estimated parts labelled.
- The person has seen the table and the date of the figures.

### Mark blocked when

- Neither you nor the person can read current prices in this session.
- The plan that fits the requirements is sold only through a sales quote and no quote exists yet. Record what to ask for.

### Summary to report

State which plans were compared and the outcome in general terms. Do not include the company's spend figures. Example: "Compared two plans for the current seat count using today's published prices. One fits within its seat limits; the other needs a sales quote."

## plan.decision: Record the decision and what would change it

### Ask

- "Based on this, I would recommend [plan and seat mix] because [reason]. Does that match your view?"
- "Who signs this off, and what would make you revisit it?"

### Do

Write a short decision record and show it.

```markdown
# Claude plan decision

Date: [date]. Figures checked on: [date] at claude.com/pricing.

## Decision
[Plan], [number] seats: [seat mix by type].

## Why
- Users: [total], of which [number] heavy.
- Required controls: [list]. [Which plan includes them.]
- Cost: [monthly / annual figure, firm and estimated parts].

## Options not chosen
- [Plan]: [one-line reason].

## Revisit when
- Seat count passes [the plan's limit, or a number the person sets].
- A new requirement appears: [for example audit logs].
- At renewal on [date].

## Approval
Recommended by [role]. To be approved by [role].
```

If the facts do not clearly favour one plan, say so and record the open question. Staying on the current plan is a valid outcome.

### Done when

- The decision record is written and the person agrees with it.
- It names who approves it and what would change it.

### Mark blocked when

- The recommendation depends on a step that is blocked (requirements unknown, no current prices, no quote).
- The person cannot name who approves the spend.

A recommendation that still needs sign-off from someone else is `done`, as long as the record names the approver.

### Summary to report

State the plan type recommended, the main reason and the trigger to revisit. Example: "Recommended staying on the current plan with a mix of seat types. Main reason: no required control is missing. Revisit if audit logs become a requirement or at renewal."
